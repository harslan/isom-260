Product thinking · 4 principles · 3 min

Before you touch a tool, answer four questions.

Problem First — a real pain, named in one sentence "students waste 40 minutes formatting resumes" beats "an AI resume app"
10× Better — not "also has AI" something that was impossible or absurd before
One Core Flow — one thing, end to end, working input → magic → output. No settings page
Ship & Learn — a link people can click beats any deck, any spec, any meeting

Builder's toolkit · pick your weapon

Four ways to build. One rule.

Lovable — full app, database, one-click publish today's default
Bolt.new — fastest to first pixel great for single-page tools
Claude / Gemini artifacts — one-file tools, zero setup no login for your users
AI IDEs (Cursor, Claude Code) — when you need real control not today

The rule: whichever one you can publish a link from in 30 minutes.

While you build

Three UX patterns every AI product needs.

Set expectations — say what it can and can't do before the first input one line under the title
Easy recovery — a way to edit, retry, or regenerate when the output is off the model WILL be wrong sometimes
User control — the AI drafts, the human approves, edits, exports Wednesday's approval gate, now in the UI

Your judges — the room — will be looking for these.
