① The Object

Model

A trained function that turns input text into a probability distribution over what comes next.

↳ you built and trained one
Parameters (weights)

The numbers inside the model that training adjusts; collectively, they are all of its "knowledge."

↳ your census: 104,475 of them
Transformer

The neural-network architecture behind modern AI — its superpower is attention, and it runs in parallel on GPUs, which is why it scales.

↳ the T in GPT; the code cell you trained
Architecture

The wiring diagram of a model — how many layers, how wide, connected how — as distinct from the weights learned inside it.

↳ block_size 16 · 64 dims · 4 heads · 2 layers

② Language In, Language Out

Token

The unit a model reads and writes — roughly ¾ of an English word in frontier models; a single letter in your TinyGPT. You are billed per token.

↳ your model: 27 possible tokens
Tokenization

Chopping text into tokens — the source of famous quirks, from letter-counting fails to some languages costing 3× more.

↳ Session 3 opens with this
Embedding

The list of numbers representing a token; similar things sit close together, so meaning is literally a location in space.

↳ your vowel map — a,e,o,u found each other
Context window

How much text the model can consider at once — its entire working memory; nothing outside it exists for the model.

↳ TinyGPT: 16 letters · Fable 5.1: 1M tokens

③ Training — How It Learns

Training data

The text a model learned its patterns from; a model can only reflect the world it was shown.

↳ 32,033 names — and why 'i' wandered off the map
Loss (cross-entropy)

The model's average surprise at the true next token — mathematically, −log of the probability it gave the right answer.

↳ the surprise meter; your 3.5 → 1.75
Gradient descent

Learning by nudging every weight a tiny step in the direction that reduces the loss — rolling downhill, millions of times.

↳ the Loan Officer's sliders moving themselves
Backpropagation

The algorithm that computes, for every weight at once, which direction is "downhill" — it's how the nudge is calculated.

↳ the one line: loss.backward()
Training step

One batch of examples → one nudge of all the weights; training is just many steps.

↳ your model: 2,501 · frontier: trillions
Seed

The fixed starting point for "random" choices; set it and every run reproduces exactly.

↳ seed 260 — why the whole class got identical names

④ Generation — How It Writes

Inference

Using a trained model, as opposed to training it — milliseconds and cents, versus months and millions. Two different economies.

↳ every generate cell you ran
Logits → softmax

The model's raw score for every possible next token (logits), squashed by softmax into probabilities that sum to 1.

↳ the crystal-ball bar chart
Sampling

Choosing the next token by rolling weighted dice over the probabilities — generation is sampling on repeat.

↳ every name your model invented
Temperature

Rescaling the probabilities before rolling — low favors the safest token (repetitive), high flattens the odds (creative, risky).

↳ Faydovinerresnylenmysa, T=2.0
Hallucination

Confident generation of plausible-but-false content — the model samples what sounds right; it does not look anything up.

↳ the invented remote-work studies in the Rater game

⑤ Becoming an Assistant

Pretraining

The massive next-token phase over internet-scale text — where raw capability comes from.

↳ your TinyGPT's training, at 10-million× scale
Instruction tuning

Fine-tuning on examples of following directions — turns an autocomplete into something that answers you.

↳ pipeline step 2, Monday
RLHF

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback: humans pick the better of two answers, a reward model learns their taste, the LLM is optimized to please it.

↳ you were the rater — and saw the side effects
Alignment

Making a model's behavior match human intent and values — a values problem wearing a math costume.

↳ sycophancy, length bias, over-refusal

⑥ Scale & The Frontier

Scaling laws

The empirical finding that capability rises predictably with more parameters, data, and compute — why labs keep going bigger.

↳ "scale is a strategy, not a detail"
Test-time compute

The second scaling dial: letting a model think longer per question — drafting, checking, revising before answering. You control it, and pay for it.

↳ reasoning models; GPT-6's benchmark harness
Benchmark

A standardized test for models — useful until it saturates, leaks into training data, or stops resembling your actual task.

↳ the 5-question BS-detector
Evaluation (eval)

Your own test, on your own task, with your own data — the only benchmark that predicts your production performance.

↳ we build these in Session 11
