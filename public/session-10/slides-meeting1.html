It has a name: rubber-stamping. A smoke alarm that goes off every time you make toast. Soon somebody takes the battery out.

Case 1 of 4 · 🚕 ride-hailing · Sept 2022
📲A hacker had an Uber contractor's password. One thing stood in the way: a prompt on the contractor's phone, "Approve this sign-in?" He sent it again and again for over an hour. Then a WhatsApp message, posing as Uber IT: approve one and they'll stop.

The contractor approved one. The hacker was inside Uber. Where was the human in the loop?

✋ a tired yes
The human was in the loop. Flooded, with nothing on the screen to judge by. That was you, three minutes ago. An approval is only as good as the attention behind it.
Uber security update, Sept 2022. Reported by Bleeping Computer and InfoQ. The technique is called MFA fatigue.
Case 2 of 4 · 🏙️ city government · March 2024
🗽New York City put an official AI chatbot online for business owners. Reporters asked: can a boss take a cut of workers' tips? Must a landlord accept housing vouchers? Polite and confident, it said yes to the tips, no to the vouchers. Follow either answer and you break the law.

Which forbidden word would have caught that?

🧱 none
No bad words. A calm, helpful tone. A wrong answer. A word list reads words. You need a reader that reads meaning.
The Markup and THE CITY, March 2024. In early 2026 the new mayor moved to shut the chatbot down.
Case 3 of 4 · 💻 software · April 2025
📧Users of Cursor, a popular coding tool, kept getting logged out. One emailed support. "Sam" wrote back: that's expected, one device per subscription, a security policy. No such policy existed. Sam was an AI. People cancelled before the company could correct it.

What should Sam have said?

🎚️ "I'm not sure"
It didn't know, and it answered anyway, in a confident voice. The most valuable sentence an agent can learn: "let me check with a person."
Cursor's co-founder confirmed it publicly. Reported by eWeek and CX Today, April 2025.
Case 4 of 4 · 💳 fintech · 2024 → 2025
🔁2024: Klarna announces its AI assistant is doing the work of 700 customer-service agents. 2025: the CEO says the cost-cutting push went too far, and Klarna starts hiring people again so a customer can always reach a human.

Did they switch the AI off?

🎚️ they moved the dial
No. The AI still handles the routine questions. They changed which conversations get a person. It was never AI or people. It's a dial.
Klarna, Feb 2024; CEO interview with Bloomberg, May 2025, as reported by Entrepreneur and eMarketer.

Today · the badge gets three upgrades

A badge that knows when to ask.

🧑‍⚖️
The second reader
A second AI that never writes. It reads each answer and says how sure it is, 0 to 100, that it can go out with nobody looking.
Like an editor, not a spell-checker.
guardrail word: output check, "LLM-as-judge"
🎚️
The dial
A number you set. At or above the bar, it goes out. Below the bar, a person reads it first.
Like a new hire who knows when to say "let me check with my manager."
guardrail word: confidence threshold
📌
Always ask
Topics that go to a person however sure the AI is. You don't leave these to a score.
Like a pharmacist who checks every prescription, even the easy ones.
guardrail word: approval gate
One line to keep: rules for what you already know. A second reader for what you don't. A person for what matters.
The dial · a confidence threshold

Every position costs something.

0 · not sure at all50100 · completely sure
🟡 Below your bar → a person reads itThe second reader wasn't sure enough. It waits in the tray.
📤 At the bar or aboveIt goes out. Nobody looks.
😬 Bar too low: something goes out that needed a person. One of those can be a lawsuit. We call it a slip.
🥱 Bar too high: your manager reads things that were fine. Hours a day. Then the rubber stamp comes back. We call it wasted.

The AI doesn't choose the bar. The business does.
